Abstract

Evaluating whether video generation models understand the physical world is essential, since physical understanding is a prerequisite for their use as world models. Existing physics benchmarks generate a video from a text prompt describing a physical scenario and score its physical plausibility. However, each scenario is represented by a single prompt, although it can be described in many semantically and physically equivalent ways. We show that this single-prompt design makes the evaluation sensitive to the choice of prompt. Using a large language model, we rewrite benchmark prompts while preserving their semantics and physics, and evaluate five video generation models on VideoPhy-2 and PhyGenBench with fixed seeds. Rewriting alone changes the physical score in 35.5% and 35.7% of cases, flips the official pass decision in 13.3%, and changes the point ranking of 4 of the 5 models on each benchmark. To address this prompt sensitivity, we propose Prompt Set Evaluation, which scores each scenario over a set of validated equivalent prompts and reports the mean score with its variation; five prompts per scenario substantially improve the stability of scores and rankings. The sensitivity is not random noise, since it persists under fully repeatable evaluators. In a blinded human study of video pairs whose scores differ, raters seldom share the evaluator's preference and judge about half of the pairs equally plausible, which suggests that the vision-language models serving as evaluators also respond to differences between videos unrelated to physics. Physical understanding should therefore be evaluated over a distribution of descriptions rather than a single prompt.

    3 · Method

    Prompt Set Evaluation

    A benchmark reports the evaluation result at one fixed description p_0 of scenario s. Prompt Set Evaluation targets the expected physical score over the distribution Q_s of valid descriptions, and estimates it with a set of K validated equivalent prompts that every model shares:

    \theta_m(s)=\mathbb{E}_{p\sim Q_s}\big[\,Y_m(s,p)\,\big]
    \widehat\theta_m(s)=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}Y_m(s,p_k)

    The spread of the K scores is reported with the mean. The benchmark's scenarios, criteria, and evaluator stay unchanged.

    1. Rewrite

      A language model rewrites the original prompt under an instruction that permits grammatical restructuring, voice changes, and conservative synonyms, and forbids adding or removing objects, quantities, actions, directions, materials, forces, camera instructions, or scene details.

    2. Validate

      Human annotators accept 95.0% of sampled rewrites and reject none; three language-model judges accept 96.7–100%. Annotators reject 80% of control rewrites in which a physical detail was altered. No video or score is seen during construction.

    3. Evaluate

      Every model is scored on the same K prompts. A different seed per prompt also covers sampling variation at no extra cost.

    How many prompts are enough?

    Left: standard error of a scenario's physical score, in percent of the score range, averaged over scenarios and models.     5 · Prompts and seeds

    Rewording a prompt matters as much as resampling the video

    On five VideoPhy-2 scenarios, CogVideoX-5B and Wan2.1-14B generated each of the six prompts with the same five new seeds. Changing the prompt at a fixed seed moves the physical-commonsense score about as much as changing the seed at a fixed prompt, yet only the seed is usually treated as a source of variation.

    6 · Recommendations

    Evaluate scenarios, not prompts

    • Benchmarks should release validated prompt sets and report the spread across prompts with the mean.
    • Five prompts per scenario, each with its own seed, cover both the choice of description and sampling variation.
    • Hosted evaluator stages flip about one decision in ten on repeat and should be scored repeatedly.
    • Prompt sets stabilize the evaluation but do not make an evaluator more faithful to physics; evaluators invariant to non-physical differences are the next step.

    Scope: two benchmarks with 15 scenarios each and five models; text-conditioned generation scored by model-based evaluators. The human study has four raters and shows that evaluator and human judgments diverge without identifying the cause.
